## The job

Eight feet is a lot of dry stone wall. At that height the wall stops being a boundary marker and becomes a piece of structure: the batter has to be right the whole way up, the hearting has to be packed properly course by course, and every through stone has to earn its place. Get it wrong and it does not show for years — and then it shows all at once.

## Walling and banker work together

The wall returns to a new gateway and ties into the older walling beyond it, so the new build had to pick up the line and the character of what was already there. Set into the face is a plaque carved with the house name — banker work, cut at the bench, then built into the wall as it went up rather than fixed on afterwards.

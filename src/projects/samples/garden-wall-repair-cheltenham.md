## The problem

An eight-metre length of a Victorian mortared garden wall had developed a serious bulge — leaning visibly over the neighbour's border, with cracked joints radiating from the distortion and copings working loose above it. The causes were textbook: decades of moisture in the core, root pressure from a since-removed tree, and pointing that had long since failed.

## What we did

1. **Careful take-down** — the failed length was dismantled by hand back to sound, plumb work, the stone stacked and sorted for reuse and the neighbour's planting protected throughout.
2. **Rebuilding in lime** — the section was rebuilt to the original coursing in a lime mortar matched to the wall, toothed into the standing work at each end so the repair carries load properly.
3. **Copings rebedded** — the coping stones along the full wall were lifted where loose and rebedded, closing the route water had been taking into the core.
4. **Repointing** — open joints along the rest of the wall were raked out and repointed, buying the sound sections decades.

## The result

Because the wall was rebuilt with its own stone and pointed in matched lime, the repaired length is effectively invisible — and structurally it's the strongest part of the wall. Refixing the copings and repointing the rest means the owner shouldn't need us again for a long time, which is how we measure a good repair.

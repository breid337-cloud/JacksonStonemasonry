## The brief

The owners of a period cottage in Broadway wanted the house name carved in stone to replace a painted timber sign — something that would suit the building, weather with it, and still be legible in fifty years. The letterforms needed to feel traditional without copying any particular period detail on the house.

## What we did

1. **Design to scale** — we drew the plaque at full size: letterforms, spacing, margins and the section of the stone itself, and agreed the drawing with the owners before touching stone. With lettering, what you approve on paper is what you get in stone.
2. **Stone selection** — a fine-grained Cotswold limestone, chosen to hold crisp letter edges while matching the elevation it would sit in.
3. **Hand carving** — the letters were cut by hand with mallet and chisel. Hand-cut V-section letters catch light and shadow in a way routed lettering doesn't — it's the difference you can't quite name but always see.
4. **Fixing** — the finished plaque was built into the front elevation, bedded and pointed in lime to match the surrounding joints.

## The result

A sign that belongs to the house — same stone, same joints, hand-worked like everything else on the frontage — and one that will still be doing its job long after any painted board would have been replaced twice.

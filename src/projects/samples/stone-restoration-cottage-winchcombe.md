## The problem

A classic case, and one we see all over the Cotswolds: a period cottage repointed in hard cement decades ago. The cement had trapped moisture in the soft limestone, and every winter's frost pushed a little more of the stone face off. Stones were eroding back behind proud fins of cement, and around the worst of it the surface was powdering away.

## What we did

1. **Raking out by hand** — the cement was removed with hand tools, joint by joint, protecting the arrises of the stone. This is slow, careful work; power tools here destroy edges in seconds.
2. **Stone repairs** — six stones had decayed beyond saving. We cut them out and pieced in matched Cotswold stone, hand-dressed to sit with the original tooling. Several more were conserved in place with discreet mortar repairs.
3. **Repointing in lime** — the elevation was repointed in a lime mortar matched to surviving original pointing in colour, texture and joint finish, then protected while it cured.

## The result

The cottage can breathe again: moisture now leaves through the lime joints instead of through the face of the stone, which stops the cycle of frost damage at its cause. Visually, the fussy grey cement grid is gone and the elevation reads as stone first, joints second — the way a Cotswold cottage should.

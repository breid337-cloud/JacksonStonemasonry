## Cut to order

This one is not a single job but something Jackson's offer as standard: house names and numbers, cut by hand in stone, to whatever specification you want.

Lettering is where masonry stops being purely structural. A carved name has to be drawn out full size before a chisel touches it — letter spacing on stone is judged by eye, not measured, because optically even spacing and mathematically even spacing are not the same thing.

## Painted or left plain

Cut lettering can be left as bare stone, where it reads by shadow alone and changes through the day, or painted so it carries at a distance. Both are shown here: the same sign before painting, and a door number left plain against ashlar.

## Round, square, built in or fixed on

Numbers are cut on discs or squares, in stone chosen to sit against the building rather than fight it. They can be built into a wall as it is laid — as with the name plaque on our [eight-foot walling job](/projects/dry-stone-wall-name-plaque/) — or fixed to a wall that is already standing.

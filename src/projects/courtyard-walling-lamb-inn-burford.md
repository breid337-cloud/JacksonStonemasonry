## The problem

A courtyard behind an old inn is a difficult place to build. Everything around it is already there and already old — mullioned windows, a cast-iron spiral stair, existing walls carrying planters — and the ground drops and turns. New work has to sit into all of it without looking bolted on.

## What we did

A new wall was built in coursed Cotswold limestone and finished with a dressed coping, set against the building and levelled off the downpipe. From its end, a curved low wall sweeps round to form the edge of a gravel bed and ties back into the existing courtyard wall.

## The result

Two pieces of new stonework doing quite different jobs — one straight and coped, one curved and low — reading as though they had always been part of the courtyard.

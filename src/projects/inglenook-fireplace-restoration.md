## The problem

The opening was sound in principle — original oak bressummer, original stone hearth — but the jambs either side had weathered to a rough, pitted face and were no longer carrying cleanly.

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/img/work-inglenook-before.jpg" alt="Cottage inglenook before restoration, with rough pitted stone jambs either side of the opening and an acrow prop supporting the beam" loading="lazy" width="1200" height="1600">
  <figcaption>Before: the old jambs, and the prop taking the beam.</figcaption>
</figure>

## What we did

The beam was propped, the worn jambs taken out, and new dressed stone set either side of the opening. The bressummer, the brick back and the stone hearth all stayed exactly where they were — the work is confined to what had actually failed.

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/img/work-inglenook-after.jpg" alt="Restored cottage inglenook with clean new dressed stone jambs beneath the original oak beam" loading="lazy" width="1200" height="1600">
  <figcaption>After: clean jambs, original beam and hearth untouched.</figcaption>
</figure>

## The result

An opening that reads as it should, without the cottage losing anything original in the process. To quote the job as Jackson's described it: to make sure this fireplace lasts another 200 years.

## The problem

Taking a chimney down on a church gable is ordinary stonemasonry. Doing it when a honeybee colony has made its home inside the wall is not.

Opening the stonework revealed comb built deep into the cavity. Bees in a wall cannot simply be worked around: disturb them and you have a welfare problem, a safety problem for anyone on the scaffold, and a colony that will try to return to the same spot.

## What we did

We worked alongside a beekeeper, in bee suits, and the colony came out before the masonry went any further — moved to another home rather than destroyed. Only then did the chimney come down and the gable get made good in matching stone.

## The result

A sound gable, a chimney safely down, and a colony of honeybees still alive somewhere else in the Cotswolds. Not every stone job needs a beekeeper on site — but when it does, it is worth doing properly.

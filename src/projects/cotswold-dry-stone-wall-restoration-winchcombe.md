## The problem

A field boundary wall on the edge of Winchcombe had been quietly failing for years: three sections had collapsed completely, several more were bulging, and most of the cope stones had been lost or knocked off. Stock had started testing the gaps, and the owner wanted the wall stock-proof again before winter.

## What we did

1. **Assessment and sorting** — we walked the full 45 metres, marked the failed and failing sections, and sorted the fallen stone into face stone, hearting and copes. Almost all of the original stone was reusable.
2. **Taking down to sound work** — the bulged lengths were dismantled back to firm, well-seated stonework, past the visible distortion, so the rebuild would tie into a solid wall.
3. **Rebuilding traditionally** — each section was rebuilt with a proper batter, tightly packed hearting and through stones at regular height — the details that decide whether a wall stands for five years or a hundred.
4. **Re-coping the full run** — the whole wall was finished with a continuous course of cope stones, locking the top and keeping water out of the core.

## The result

A stock-proof wall, rebuilt in its own stone, that reads as one continuous piece of walling — the repaired sections weathering to match the rest within a couple of seasons. Because the failure causes (poor hearting and a lost coping) were dealt with rather than patched over, this is a repair measured in decades, not years.

## The problem

A cill takes the water that runs off the whole window, so it is usually the first part of a stone surround to go. This one had weathered back badly, with the mullion above it breaking up and the bed joints opening.

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/img/work-window-mullion-before.jpg" alt="Eroded stone window cill and mullion, weathered back with open joints" loading="lazy" width="1280" height="1600">
  <figcaption>Before: cill and mullion eroded and breaking up.</figcaption>
</figure>

## What we did

The failed stone was cut out and new dressed stone pieced in, worked to the same profile as the original so the repair looks like part of the window rather than a patch on it.

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/img/work-window-mullion-after.jpg" alt="Repaired window with a new stone cill and mullion in fresh honey-coloured limestone" loading="lazy" width="1280" height="1600">
  <figcaption>After: new cill and mullion, crisp-edged and ready to weather in.</figcaption>
</figure>

## The result

The opening sheds water properly again. The new stone stands out for now, as new Cotswold stone always does — it tones down within a few seasons.

## Stonemasonry in Gloucester

Gloucester is one of England's great historic cities — a cathedral city with Roman origins and a building stock that runs from medieval stone through Georgian brick to Victorian villas and modern estates. Stone is everywhere once you look: boundary and garden walls, gate piers, steps, cills and lintels, church and chapel stonework, and older stone cottages in the villages that ring the city.

We work across Gloucester regularly from our base in Winchcombe, about half an hour away.

## What we do in Gloucester

- **Stone restoration** — repointing in lime mortar, matched stone replacement and repairs on period stonework across the city and its conservation areas.
- **Garden and boundary wall repair** — collapsed, bulging and vehicle-damaged stone walls rebuilt, reusing the original stone. See [stone wall repair](/stone-wall-repair/).
- **Stone cleaning** — sympathetic cleaning of soot, grime, algae and staining from stone elevations, walls and steps — chosen to suit the stone, never blasted.
- **Dry stone walling** — new and rebuilt dry stone walls on the Cotswold side of the city and in the surrounding countryside.
- **New stonework and carving** — dressed stone features, repairs to cills and lintels, and hand-carved house signs and date stones.

## A note on Gloucester's stone

Unlike the uniformly limestone towns higher up in the Cotswolds, Gloucester's older stonework is mixed: Cotswold limestone brought down from the hills, softer local stones, and sandstone in some church and Victorian work. Matching the right stone — and the right mortar — matters even more where materials vary, and it's the first thing we establish on any repair.

Gloucester itself plus the surrounding towns and villages, including Barnwood, Hucclecote, Brockworth, Churchdown, Longlevens, Quedgeley, Hardwicke, Upton St Leonards, Painswick and the Cotswold edge villages between Gloucester and Cheltenham.

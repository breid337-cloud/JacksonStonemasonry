## Stonemasonry in Tewkesbury

Tewkesbury is best known for its magnificent Norman abbey and its medieval timber-framed streets — but stone runs right through the town and the villages around it. Garden and boundary walls, plinths beneath timber frames, church and churchyard stonework, and Cotswold stone cottages in the villages towards the hills all need the same traditional care.

We're based in Winchcombe, roughly 20 minutes away, and cover Tewkesbury and its surrounding villages regularly.

## What we do around Tewkesbury

- **Stone wall repair** — collapsed and leaning garden and boundary walls rebuilt with the original stone; roadside walls repaired after vehicle strikes, with the paperwork insurers need. See [stone wall repair](/stone-wall-repair/).
- **Stone restoration** — lime repointing, stone replacement and repairs to period stonework, including work on listed buildings and in the town's conservation area.
- **Stone cleaning** — algae, moss and grime lifted carefully from walls, steps and elevations; particularly relevant in a riverside town where damp, shaded stonework greens up quickly.
- **Dry stone walling** — new walls and rebuilds in the villages on the Cotswold side: Alderton, Ashchurch, Beckford, Bredon Hill villages and towards Winchcombe.
- **New stonework and carving** — stone features for extensions and gardens, and hand-carved signs and date stones.
- **[House signs and numbers](/house-signs/)** — hand-carved house names, numbers and date stones for Tewkesbury and the villages along the Severn and Avon.

## Flooding and stonework

Tewkesbury's relationship with water is famous, and it shows in the stonework. Walls and plinths that flood or sit damp for long periods suffer in particular ways: washed-out joints, cycles of salt crystallisation as they dry, and accelerated frost damage. Two things matter most in repairing them — lime mortar, which lets the wall dry out through its joints, and patience: repointing stone that hasn't fully dried simply locks the moisture in. We specify repairs around how the wall actually lives, not just how it looks on a dry day.

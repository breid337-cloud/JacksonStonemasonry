## Cleaning stone without damaging it

Cleaning stonework is one of those jobs where the wrong approach does permanent harm. Cotswold stone is a soft, porous limestone; blast it with high-pressure water or grit, or wash it with acid, and you strip away the weathered surface — the "case-hardened" face that has been protecting the stone for decades. The building looks raw for a season and then decays faster than it ever did before.

So our starting point is always the gentlest method that will do the job, and a test patch in a discreet corner before anything else. Sometimes the honest advice is that stonework doesn't need cleaning at all — a naturally weathered wall is part of a building's character — and we'll tell you that too.

## What we clean

- **House frontages and elevations** — lifting general atmospheric dirt and grime from Cotswold stone and other masonry.
- **Algae, moss and biological growth** — the green and black organic growth common on shaded, damp elevations and garden walls.
- **Garden walls, steps and paving** — stone boundary walls, terraces, steps and flags made slippery or unsightly by organic growth.
- **Staining** — rust runs, copper staining, and staining from fixings and rainwater goods, treated with poultices and appropriate chemical cleaners.
- **Paint and coating removal** — carefully removing old masonry paint and failed sealers from stone, where the stone beneath is sound enough to take it.
- **Post-repair cleaning** — blending cleaned and repaired areas so an elevation reads as one surface again.

## How we choose the method

There is no single "stone cleaning" technique — there is a ladder of methods, and the skill is knowing where to stop:

1. **Dry brushing and hand cleaning** — for loose dirt and growth; the least aggressive option and often enough for garden walls.
2. **Low-pressure water washing** — controlled washing that softens and lifts dirt without driving water into the wall or eroding the surface.
3. **Steam / superheated water cleaning** — highly effective on biological growth and greasy urban grime, using very little pressure and very little water; usually our preferred method on soft limestone.
4. **Biocide treatments** — killing algae and lichen at the root so regrowth is slowed, used selectively and appropriately.
5. **Poultices and specialist chemical cleaners** — for specific stains, always tested first and always neutralised properly.

What we don't do is aggressive high-pressure blasting or acid washing of soft limestone. If someone quotes you a suspiciously cheap price to "jet wash" a Cotswold stone house, that's what you're being quoted for — and the stone will pay for it later.

## When cleaning is worth doing

Cleaning makes the biggest difference when:

- a sale or refurbishment makes first impressions matter;
- biological growth is holding moisture against the stone (damp, shaded elevations);
- steps, paths and copings have become slippery and unsafe;
- staining or old paint is disfiguring an otherwise sound elevation;
- restoration work has left a patchwork that needs blending.

Cleaning also reveals the true condition of the stonework — it often pairs naturally with [repointing and restoration](/stone-restoration/), and we can quote for both together so the wall is only scaffolded and worked on once.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can you clean old stone without damaging it?**
Yes — provided the method is matched to the stone and the soiling, and tested first. That is exactly why we start with a test patch and the gentlest effective technique, and why we avoid high pressure and acids on soft limestone altogether.

**Will the algae come back?**
On a damp, shaded wall, eventually — that's its environment. A biocide treatment slows regrowth considerably, and dealing with the moisture source (overhanging vegetation, leaking gutters, poor drainage) slows it further. We'll point out anything like that while we're on site.

**Can you remove paint from stone?**
Usually, yes, using appropriate paint removal systems rather than blasting. The condition of the stone underneath matters, so we assess and test before quoting.

**How much does stone cleaning cost?**
It depends on area, access, soiling and method, so we quote per job after seeing the stonework — a free written quotation, with a test patch where useful so you can see the result before committing.
